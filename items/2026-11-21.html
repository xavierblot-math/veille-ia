Terence Tao — Unforced Euler singularity, unforced Navier-Stokes singularity, and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis

  • Tao flags three AI-assisted announcements, all built around Levent Alpöge, and ranks them by how well understood they are so far.
  • Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster and Matei Coiculescu: a readable proof, formalized in Lean, that the unforced 3D incompressible Euler equations can blow up in finite time.
  • Alpöge and Buckmaster: a Lean-verified proof of finite-time blowup for the unforced 3D Navier–Stokes equations. The method is one of the expected routes: build an approximate discretely self-similar solution numerically, then use rigorous perturbation theory to get an exact one. The written proof still needs proper professional exposition.
  • Alpöge, using an in-house AI model: a huge Lean-only proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. A Lean Zulip thread is working to check it and pull out the strategy, which at first glance relies on the full modern Langlands and arithmetic-geometry machinery.
  • 👉 If these hold up, this is the biggest AI-and-mathematics news so far. Note that the unforced case is exactly what the earlier OpenAI Navier–Stokes result left open. Worth following the Zulip thread.

Proofs and Prompts (Matt Kerr) — Artificial mathematics

  • A reply to Strogatz's Atlantic essay, which Kerr thinks ends too resignedly. He sees AI deepening a split between applied mathematics, where it is just another tool, and pure mathematics, which is facing a real sense of loss.
  • His main claim: pure mathematics is a human creation, more like composing poetry within a grammar of forms than discovering external truths. A Platonist "out there" view is therefore anti-humanistic, and handing the work to machines throws away the point of the subject.
  • He accepts AI for admin, LaTeX/TikZ and trying out examples, but not for writing papers or recommendation letters. He also urges tenured mathematicians to publish less and value good exposition.
  • He is critical of OpenAI's dump of 700+ unreviewed manuscripts (the "slop drop") and of the new LLM-oriented preprint server Hexagon. He ends by telling students not to give up on becoming mathematicians.

Proofs and Prompts (Sinisa Slijepcevic) — Letter to young mathematicians

  • An optimistic answer to the Leiden Declaration, the Fields Medallists' declaration and similar letters. He calls the present "creative destruction" and an opportunity, with three messages: embrace AI, be bolder, serve humanity. The letter was written with Claude's help, and the chat is linked.
  • Value should no longer come from technical difficulty alone: a statement an AI proves from a single prompt may count only as a lemma. He wants collaborative, AI-assisted research with credit designed in from the start (building on Po-Ling Loh's proposals).
  • Be bolder: the forced Navier–Stokes blowup proves little about real fluid dynamics. He disputes Tao's claim that AI is "depleting" the supply of good problems and argues it can help generate new questions and rank them.
  • Serve humanity: he reads AlphaFold as a triumph of mathematical structure (geometry, SE(3) invariance, probability) more than of learning. He points to the missing rigorous foundations of QFT (citing Seiberg, Connes–Kreimer, Hairer) and to clinical statistics as places mathematicians are needed.
  • 👉 The QFT and renormalization points touch mathematical physics. The footnotes are also a handy timeline of the 2026 AI results.
