Proofs and Prompts (David Cimasoni) — Salvaging that which is dearest

  • A personal essay by a Geneva senior lecturer, written without AI, on what he instinctively wants to protect as a mathematician. He frames recent months as cycling weekly through the stages of grief.
  • Societal analogies: AI is to thinking what industrial food is to nutrition (we need a balanced "AI diet", Tao's phrase) and what social media is to socializing. The risk is fast, brutal cognitive deskilling, so education must defend the right to make mistakes (he cites Grothendieck).
  • He rejects the idea that mathematics can certify a "safe level" of AI use: that bound is too high to be useful and could give false rigour. Instead, maths matters because it trains reflective thinking, models clear and honest debate, and its real product is human understanding (Thurston's essay and MathOverflow post).
  • He admits he long believed research would be the last activity affected, and now considers shifting much of his energy from research to helping younger people stay cognitively independent. A longer French version exists.

Proofs and Prompts (Ignacio Barros) — Mathematics and mysticism: another defense of human mathematics

  • An Antwerp assistant professor argues that a neglected side of mathematics is under threat: its "mystical" side, a relationship with mystery that he compares to myth and religion, the other great users of symbols.
  • He disputes the comforting view that it does not matter who creates new mathematics as long as humans appreciate it. Creation comes before understanding, and if humans stop giving life to new objects, human mathematics eventually withers.
  • Conjectures matter less as trophies than as reminders that the community lives surrounded by the unknown. Most research time is spent confused, and that is the point.
  • 👉 Barros works on the algebraic geometry of moduli spaces of curves, close to your field. A short, lyrical piece.
