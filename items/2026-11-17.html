Proofs and Prompts (Michael Goldman) — Do We Still Need Mathematical Journals?

  • Part of the publishing series. A CNRS research director (LAMA, Paris-Est Créteil) answers his title question with a clear "no".
  • He takes Michael Clarke's five functions of journals in turn. arXiv already handles dissemination and registration. Validation was always shaky, and he now trusts an LLM audit more than a human referee's. Filtration happens through arXiv, colleagues and seminars long before publication, given median AMS acceptance delays of 12–17 months.
  • That leaves designation, a proxy for hiring and promotion, which the IMU, CNRS and DORA statements already reject. He also points to a seldom-mentioned role: acceptance is one of the few pieces of positive feedback in academic life, which may matter for colleagues' mental health.
  • Cost: about €78M a year of French public money goes on subscriptions, roughly half of it to Elsevier, which he says would pay for basic LLM plans for every French researcher. As alternatives he names arXiv plus Hexagon, We Need ABP, the Bourgain Project, and Gowers-style platforms where preprints are discussed. He will stop pushing co-authors to submit, but advises non-tenured people not to follow his example.
  • 👉 A French, CNRS-centred view that bears directly on refereeing and on career advice for students.

Proofs and Prompts (Eric Dolores-Cuenca) — Update on a new type of paper mill

  • A Yonsei postdoc follows up on his earlier post about AI-enabled paper mills. He moves the focus to what editors and institutions can do.
  • He compares the situation to the Wells Fargo scandal, where pressure on workers led to fraud and only a change of culture fixed it. In mathematics, he cites Clarivate dropping the field from its 2023 highly-cited list over paid-affiliation schemes.
  • He points to the IMU–ICIAM report and recommendations on fraudulent publishing (Notices AMS, Oct. 2025; arXiv:2509.09877) as the right tools against paper mills. Hiring that counts only SCIE papers still creates the bad incentives.
