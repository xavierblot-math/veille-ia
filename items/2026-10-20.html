1. Terence Tao — A sketch of the proof of non-existence of Siegel zeroes

  • Context: OpenAI's October release contains two papers proving a quasi-Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions (bounds 11/12 and 7/8), plus a third, 9-page paper ruling out Siegel zeros directly via algebraic number theory and the geometry of numbers.
  • What Tao does: presents Ben Green's simplification of that third paper (Banks and Kerr have also written digests).
  • Method: a lattice of two-variable integer polynomials, evaluated with horizontal derivatives on a skew grid in Z[√D]; its squared covolume M is a nonzero integer. Hadamard bounds M from above; for non-residue primes, Frobenius puts the grid p-adically close to z₁ᵖ = z₂, forcing high p-divisibility. Comparing gives an effective bound.
  • Green's novelty: uses vanishing with multiplicity, avoiding the auxiliary extension Z[√D, √2].
  • Tao's remark: the parity barrier is broken here by the polynomial method; in the QRH papers by a still poorly understood "automorphic miracle".

2. Proofs and Prompts — Recommendations on the use of AI for PhD students and supervisors

  • By a collective of ~36 symplectic geometers (incl. Seidel, Auroux, I. Smith, Viterbo, Oancea, Damian).
  • Students: ignoring AI isn't an option, but check every step; declare AI use and keep prompts in a "lab notebook"; AI is never a co-author; don't feed collaborators' unpublished work to commercial models without consent.
  • Theses: more open-ended topics, higher bar for independence and breadth; avoid being "one-on-one with the AI".
  • Supervisors: check problems against what AI solves quickly; defences should verify full mastery of the results.
  • Institutions: pay for tools; set clear disclosure rules.
  • 👉 Close to your community — relevant if you supervise students.

3. Proofs and Prompts — Contribution, Credit, and Community (T. Moraschini)

  • Premise: AI adoption is inevitable — if a button settles a conjecture, someone will press it.
  • Priority of proof should stop counting as merit on its own, or we get "Prompt or Perish" and secrecy.
  • AI disclosures are unverifiable; ranking work by them would reward concealment.
  • Proposes evaluation through talks, interviews and honest letters, and larger group collaborations.

4. Proofs and Prompts — Thinking Hard Above AI (B. M. Bumpus)

  • LLMs as a cultural technology: an interface to accumulated mathematical knowledge.
  • "Maths above AI": treat the AI's proof as a baseline, then look for the canonical proof explaining why.
  • Uses AI as a sparring partner for learning and lecture prep.
  • Optimistic about mathematics, less so about academia's metrics.
