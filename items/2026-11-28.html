Proofs and Prompts (Stan Letovsky) — Proofs, Programs, and Comprehensibility

  • An AI researcher (PhD 1988, later bioinformatics) responds to the complaint that the proofs recently released by OpenAI lack insight, drawing on his 1980s thesis on automated program understanding, which recognised known "motifs" in programs by rewriting them in lambda calculus.
  • Main proposal: via Curry–Howard and Lean, treat comprehensibility as compression, i.e. proof length relative to a shared library, plus internal deduplication by introducing definitions for repeated patterns (a minimum-description-length view); AI proofs may be opaque partly because they fail to introduce such definitions.
  • He points to Aksenov et al., "Compression is all you need: Modeling Mathematics" (March 2026), where interesting lemmas are rare and valuable because they compress the search space, and suggests insight may concern future results more than existing ones.
  • Practical suggestion: experts could prompt LLMs to rewrite the OpenAI proofs against explicit quality criteria, leading to shared meta-prompts, with bodies like AHM and AGMAI protecting the community's interests. He also frames the pushback as another case of moving the Turing-test goalposts.
