Proofs and Prompts (Nihar Gargava) — It's a great time to be an amateur mathematician

  • A Paris-Saclay postdoc reflects on outsiders producing real results with AI. Opening story (told by Dinesh Thakur at a TIFR "AI for Math" meeting): a French car mechanic, who didn't know what a finite field was, used Claude to disprove a Thakur conjecture on Carlitz–Wieferich primes (arXiv:2607.15305).
  • His own "Ramanujan–Hardy moment": Michael Simkin, an ex-IMO medallist and Game of Life constructor with no university training, sent him a messy AI write-up giving a new lower bound for lattice sphere packings in high dimensions. It became a joint paper with a PhD student (arXiv:2610.12619).
  • Argues that professionals should engage with some of these hobbyists as a form of outreach. He advises hobbyists to learn how mathematicians actually communicate, and professionals to respect hobbyists' ambitions.
  • 👉 Paris-based author; a concrete model for handling the AI-assisted "outsider" emails that are becoming common.

Proofs and Prompts (Chenmin Sun) — Nothing More than Tools?

  • A CNRS PDE researcher (UPEC) describes his journey from treating LLMs as cheap helpers to an "existential crisis", triggered by GPT-5.6 and by OpenAI's 6 October release of manuscripts grouped into 372 result families.
  • Criticises the addictive practice of "drawing cards" on open problems with agent swarms, and asks who deserves authorship when the work comes from nearly context-free prompting.
  • His answer is "toolization": the model handles bounded local tasks (estimates, numerics, case checks, learning other fields), while the human steers the key choices: blow-up profiles, ansätze, norms, Lyapunov functionals. If the model supplies a key idea, he would credit it as a collaborator.
  • Suggests regular self-checks against deskilling, e.g. whether he can still do AI-free mathematics on a plane.

Proofs and Prompts (Alessio Figalli, Sobhan Seyfaddini) — Postdoc Hiring in the Age of AI: What Still Matters

  • Two ETH professors, first entry in a series of advice from hiring committees: AI does not change the goal, which is to judge depth of understanding, the candidate's own contribution and potential.
  • Publication counts are not a reliable signal; letters, CVs and a close reading of selected papers matter for shortlisting. The interview (a talk plus in-depth discussion of what failed and why the arguments work) is decisive.
  • 👉 Directly useful when advising your PhD students on postdoc applications this season.
