Proofs and Prompts (Ishaun Datta) — The Word of God

  • A Columbia postdoc responds to Logan Graves's essay "Priest, Monk, and Mathematician", which predicts mathematicians will first become interpreters of AI-produced proofs ("priests") and then mere contemplators ("monks") once models explain themselves.
  • Personal angle: where his PhD years went into building his own proof programme, his postdoc hours now go into decoding a messy but promising GPT-6 Astra proof that builds on his work but is not his.
  • Main argument, via the Bhagavad Gita: even divine revelation comes out obscure, because truth far beyond the listener's grasp only survives as a lossy approximation in language. If AI lets research ambitions scale up, its output may stay hard to absorb, so the translator role need not be temporary.
  • He frames the "monk" scenario as an empirical question to be tested against future models rather than a foregone conclusion.

Proofs and Prompts (A.T.) — Math, Beauty, and Ego

  • A UK undergraduate retraces the run of AI milestones during their degree (AlphaGeometry, IMO gold, Erdős problems, the Jacobian conjecture via Claude Fable 5, then OpenAI's claimed Navier–Stokes blow-up in September 2026) and describes the grief that followed.
  • Core claim: mathematics bundles contemplation of beauty with ego and ambition (Hardy, Conway, and a recent Gowers post are cited). AI threatens the achievement side far more than the beauty side.
  • The essay resists reducing everything at stake to vanity: wanting to struggle with hard problems and leave a mark is part of being human. It closes on an exchange with a harmonic-analysis professor, who notes the "thin line between ambition … and pride".
  • 👉 A candid student's-eye view, useful context for thinking about PhD supervision and the motivations of younger mathematicians.
