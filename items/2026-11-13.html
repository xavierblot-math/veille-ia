Proofs and Prompts (Vincent Runge) — On AI and Progress in Mathematics: Dreaming a Mathematical Universal Machine

  • Runge (Université d'Évry Paris-Saclay, LaMME) condenses his preprint: separate the job of papers (helping humans understand) from the job of measuring progress, which would go to a shared public AI system, the "Mathematical Universal Machine".
  • He models the AI as a miner made of maps (literature, theorem databases), tools (Lean, computer algebra) and an LLM "brain", working on a finite compute budget. Its "horizon" is the set of known results it proves with probability above a threshold once those results are withheld from it.
  • A contribution would then be scored by how far it pushes that horizon after a periodic retraining. Mathematicians would work either as supervisors steering the machine or as "miners" exploring beyond its convex hull of reachable results.
  • The proposal is political: open weights, equal compute for every user, published energy accounting, consent before private work is ingested, and governance through UNESCO/IMU, as an alternative to company-owned models.
  • 👉 A concrete French proposal for evaluating research and for academic AI infrastructure, worth reading in full.

Proofs and Prompts (anonymous) — Working with the AI Companies is a Losing Strategy

  • An untenured North American assistant professor argues that OpenAI ignored the central demand of its advisory group AGMAI: stop testing open problems on internal models that outsiders cannot use. The author points to the 6 October batch of results as proof.
  • The author calls on AGMAI to dissolve. Mathematicians, they say, should stop giving the companies training data, free publicity and institutional legitimacy (citations, enthusiastic quotes to the press).
  • This is not a rejection of AI itself: the post backs building community-owned AI infrastructure, with fair access and proper credit, following Tao and others.
  • The author admits it is hard to set aside the excitement of new results, but argues that institutional survival comes first.

Proofs and Prompts (David Solomon) — Sharing two worries about AI

  • Solomon (associate researcher at IMJ, Paris) names two parts of research he loves: building and fighting with one's own project, and sharing partial ideas with colleagues.
  • His first worry is that sharing (talks, emails, arXiv preprints) now risks someone feeding his half-formed ideas into an LLM, which would take away the satisfaction of finishing the work himself. So he is tempted to share much less, which would hurt the field if many react the same way.
  • His second point is that the debate should link mathematics' troubles to the wider risk of humans losing control over human activities. He sees the profession's anxiety as an early example of that larger risk.
  • 👉 A Paris colleague's view on how AI could erode open sharing of ideas, which matters for collaboration and supervision.
