Proofs and Prompts (Francesco Fournier-Facio, Yara Kyrychenko, Jon Roozenbeek, Julian Wykowski) — Futures of Maths Launch (also on Tao's blog)

  • Launch of Futures of Maths, an ongoing online consultation asking active research mathematicians how AI is changing their research and teaching, and where the field is heading.
  • Participants rate short statements on maths and AI on an agree/disagree scale, flag the ones they consider most important, see aggregate ratings afterwards, and can submit their own (moderated) statements, so the question set evolves.
  • Eligibility: at least one zbMATH-indexed journal article in the last five years, checked via ORCID/Crossref (or a manual review by institutional email). Verification is kept separate so the survey stays anonymous; you get a personal return link that cannot be resent.
  • Results will be published on Proofs and Prompts once enough responses are in; the organisers ask for wide and diverse participation.
  • 👉 You qualify as a participant, and it takes only a few minutes; worth passing on to colleagues and students in Paris too.

Proofs and Prompts (anonymous math PhD student) — I'm Not Done Doing Math! Don't Ignore Me!

  • Part of the series on publishing and credit. The author, a soon-to-graduate PhD with no papers and no research-postdoc prospects, argues that AI makes it realistic to keep doing research alongside a teaching load or a day job.
  • Main complaint: both pro- and anti-AI voices on the blog ignore people outside research positions; summits and society norm-setting are run by already successful mathematicians.
  • Pushes back on several proposals: if referees are scarce, the many PhDs who leave academia could review; replacing papers with seminars and workshops would exclude those without travel funding; fears of maths becoming a "hobby" ignore decades of a brutal job market.
  • 👉 A useful perspective for PhD supervision: what research life after a weaker PhD might look like with AI tools.
