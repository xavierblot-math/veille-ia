Terence Tao — Doctoral Advising Guidelines by the AHM

  • A guest post by the AHM communications group, reposting the announcement already covered yesterday from Proofs and Prompts.
  • It points to the Association for Human Mathematics' five recommendations on doctoral advising: human-authored writing, limits on reliance on AI, the ethics of AI use, and student wellbeing.
  • Now that it is on Tao's blog, it will reach a much wider audience, and the comments there may be worth a look.

Proofs and Prompts (Marco Trombetti) — Keeping Mathematical Conferences Human

  • Argument: AI makes polished slides, scripts and ready-made answers cheap, but understanding is not getting cheaper. A smooth talk therefore says less and less about whether the speaker understands the work.
  • Proposal: talks as an interview. A chair reads the paper beforehand and keeps interrupting (why this definition, where is the hard step, what is the simplest example), and the audience can join in throughout.
  • He takes the stress objection seriously, especially for junior speakers and non-native English speakers. Possible fixes: questions shared in advance, questions that explain rather than attack, and keeping classic talks as an option.
  • Bigger picture: conferences as a soft stage of "humanization" and verification, where someone has to sit beside a result and discuss it. He suggests trying one session of five speakers before changing anything more widely.
  • 👉 A concrete, testable idea that a seminar or workshop organiser could try.

Proofs and Prompts (Isabelle Gallagher) — Slow Maths

  • The president of the Société Mathématique de France calls AI use a kind of "doping". It went from checking bibliographies to fleets of agents "cracking" thesis problems and even Clay problems.
  • She ties this to older problems: the cult of publication counts, prizes concentrated on a few stars, and the race to publish driven by competition and lack of funding. She also raises the environmental cost, which she says nobody is addressing.
  • Her answer is "slow maths": value the process, the detours, the writing, the explaining and the collective work, credit ideas and proofreading as much as the techniques that finish a proof, and step out of the publication race together.
  • 👉 The SMF's leadership taking a position. This is central to the debate in the French mathematical community.

Proofs and Prompts (Dora Woodruff, Hanna Mularczyk, Anna Brandenberger) — Our Survey of Early Career Mathematicians

  • Three MIT graduate students surveyed PhD students and postdocs about AI: over 260 responses from about 60 institutions in 5 countries, with many long free-text answers. The results are on arXiv.
  • They single out Questions 3.2, 3.3 and 5.2 as surprising or worrying. Almost all answers came in before 6 October, and they suspect the picture has shifted since then.
  • They ask journals, hiring committees and faculty to take these results into account and to keep talking with junior researchers.
  • 👉 Useful context for PhD supervision.
