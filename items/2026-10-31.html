Proofs and Prompts (Freed, Guth, Kra, Riehl, Abouzaid) — Recommendations from the Harvard CMSA summit on Math and AI (also on Tao's blog)

  • Report of a two-day summit at Harvard CMSA where 32 mathematicians from academia, government and industry met to plan for AI's impact on research.
  • Five working groups: evaluation/production/dissemination of research, coordinated action, open-source tools, formalization, and academia–AI lab collaboration; the full report and a feedback form are on the summit website.
  • The organizers stress that the evaluation section contains suggestions departments can apply now to hiring and review, while acknowledging a temporary extra load on faculty, justified by the need to protect students and junior colleagues.
  • 👉 Directly relevant to hiring and PhD supervision; worth reading the report itself. (The Tao blog copy has a "__trashed" URL, so it may have been withdrawn there.)

Proofs and Prompts (Anonymous early-career researcher) — Math is for Feeding your Family

  • A tenure-track mathematician says that in one month GPT Pro let them settle so many open problems they could write ten papers a semester, and asks bluntly what tenure-worthy research now means.
  • Argues that philosophical talk about "mathematics is understanding" dodges the practical question of how output is evaluated, and that journals cannot survive.
  • Offers a provocative candidate policy: abolish journals, organize credit around live workshops where humans digest AI results, and hire on teaching, mentoring and service.

Proofs and Prompts (Nicola Gigli, SISSA) — The value of mathematicians in the AI age

  • Part of the series on publishing and credit. Gigli assumes research will largely be automated and argues the lasting value of mathematicians is human understanding of mathematics, as the language of science and a safeguard of control over technology.
  • Once research gets cheap, academia's value system, built on papers and authorship, collapses; he proposes valuing knowing, understanding and communicating mathematics instead.
  • Sees a future role for humans as guides ("Cicerone") who build narratives and maps through an ocean of theorems, and worries about who the profession will attract next.

Proofs and Prompts (Patrick Morris, UPC Barcelona) — Let's stop writing papers…

  • A postdoc argues for moving away from journal papers toward a diverse ecosystem of output, while admitting junior careers still depend on publication lists.
  • Five axes, A–E: Atlasing (a living encyclopedia of results), Big Mathematics (large open collaborations, including logs of failures), Curation, Dialogue, and Exposition.
  • Points to existing initiatives (Mathlib, the video journal Mathematical Discourse, Quanta's "Transformation" digest, Mathathon) and calls for prestigious venues and community-owned infrastructure that reward these activities.

Terence Tao — Collaborative retreat: New developments in the theory of zeta functions

  • Announcement of a retreat at the Hausdorff Center in Bonn, 11–15 January, organized by Assing, Blomer and Brumley, for early-career analytic number theorists.
  • Centered on the recent proof of a quasi-Riemann Hypothesis over the rationals and number fields, and its consequences (Artin's primitive root conjecture, class group torsion, equidistribution of torus orbits).
