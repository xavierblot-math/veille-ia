Terence Tao — On the diameter and distribution of Sidon sets

  • New arXiv preprint with Daniel Carter and Kevin O'Bryant improving the upper bound on the size of Sidon sets in {1,…,N} (Erdős problem #30); it grew out of a blog discussion last year about AlphaEvolve improving the record.
  • Method: the distinct-differences property gives a convolution inequality, tested against carefully chosen weight and autocorrelation functions; replacing sharp cutoffs by optimised (and finally random) cutoffs lowers the constant in the second-order term.
  • Tao says the method seems to have hit its limit, and a slightly sharper constant (0.942809522) was just obtained by a different approach; the authors deliberately did not use current AI tools to optimise their own bound.
  • The technique also extends to generalised Golomb rulers, where each difference may repeat a bounded number of times.

Proofs and Prompts (Maximilian Kaipel) — Why and how to transparently disclose AI use in mathematics research

  • Part of the publishing/credit series. Notes that about a quarter of August 2026 math arXiv papers disclosed AI use, with very uneven detail, and proposes a standard.
  • Three principles: only humans can take responsibility and so be authors; disclosures should name the exact model, the task delegated and where in the paper (e.g. Lemma 3.5); all AI interactions that shaped the paper should be archived in full.
  • Offers a taxonomy (MAIDeT) of delegable tasks in five groups (knowledge acquisition, objective identification, proof construction, computation, presentation), plus a LaTeX package, mathaidisclose, that generates the disclosure section from per-environment commands.
  • Also suggests that mathematicians who refuse AI state this explicitly in their papers.
  • 👉 Practical and ready to use; relevant for your own papers and for PhD students.

Proofs and Prompts (Johannes Schmitt) — Math2038: A Day in the Life of Future Mathematicians

  • A short story set at ETH in 2038: frontier research is done by agent workflows feeding a vast "MathNet", while humans teach, digest decade-old AI results into monographs (an Oberwolfach Arbeitsgemeinschaft on Lagrangian fibrations), and do AI-free research retreats as training.
  • Imagines hiring based on an in-person "Mathematical Profile Certificate" exam replacing reference letters, since publications can no longer be trusted as signals.
  • Ends on mathematics as something still pursued for its own sake, like amateur marathon running alongside faster robots.
  • 👉 Light reading with a symplectic flavour (hyperkähler and Lagrangian fibration references).

Proofs and Prompts (Masoud Kamgarpour) — Responsibility of Mathematicians in the Age of AI

  • A personal essay: the author concluded by July that models outdid him at mathematics, and describes the Navier–Stokes announcements as a shock to the community.
  • Argues mathematicians should help find language for what AI is doing (e.g. intelligence being "grown" rather than built) and convey the speed of progress to the public.
  • Endorses the recent Royal Society mathematicians' letter and calls for public mobilisation to slow AI development, while keeping room for traditional, AI-free mathematics.
