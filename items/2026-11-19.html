Proofs and Prompts (Anonymous) — Why the Future of Mathematics Belongs to Human Judgment

  • Thesis: once AI can produce correct theorems and new conjectures at scale, the scarce resource is no longer proofs but human attention, so judging which results matter becomes the mathematician's central job.
  • Picks up Tao's "Math 2.0" idea: the "conjecture era" built on problems humans chose ends, and AI companies are blamed for trading on famous conjectures while ignoring why the community valued them.
  • Draws on Poincaré's "Mathematical Creation" (invention as discernment) and Liu Cixin's story The Poetry Cloud, where generating every possible poem still fails to pick out the good ones.
  • Pictures mathematicians as "cartographers of significance" mapping machine output, and urges people to choose problems they themselves value rather than chase the conjectures AI labs advertise; links earlier P&P posts on AI and PhD supervision.

Proofs and Prompts (Tiago Veríssimo) — Mathematics: From Deductive Science to Actually an Experimental Science

  • A Newcastle PhD student argues that as AI takes over theorem proving, human work shifts to "mathematical experimentation": exploring phenomena that strike you as curious and handing the results to an AI workflow that formalises them.
  • He separates computational experiments (working out examples) from "conceptual experiments", hunches tested in dialogue with AI, which he likens to vibe coding.
  • Suggests judging researchers, and even hiring, grants and journal reports, on the taste behind their lines of experiment; he reads today's unease as an "epistemic flip" against career incentives built around proving theorems.
