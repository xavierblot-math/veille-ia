AGMAI (incl. Gowers), on Tao's blog and Proofs and Prompts — Disadvantages of Designating Mathematicians for AI Output Releases (Tao's blog)

  • The Advisory Group (Charles, De Lellis, Gowers, Hairer, Srivastava, Tillmann, Vakil, Witten, Wood) clarifies its 29 September recommendations. AI labs should stop handing their output to hand-picked mathematicians to check and present.
  • Five reasons: unequal early access, the community losing control over who does the work, selection that reflects the labs' own networks, the mathematicians' reputations shielding the lab from criticism, and the fact that recent releases show the community can do this unaided.
  • Instead they ask labs to post on independent repositories, give full details of the process (model, prompts, attempts, compute, cost), formalize in Lean where possible, and fund follow-up workshops chosen by independent nonprofits.
  • 👉 Gowers and François Charles (ENS) among the signatories.

Proofs and Prompts (Anonymous) — Use LLMs to check your work

  • Opens a new series on publishing and credit. A referee argues there is now a professional duty to run papers through an LLM before submitting them, for both copy-editing and finding mathematical errors.
  • Main argument: obvious, easily caught mistakes waste referees' limited time. Objections about AI labs or energy use are answered by open-weight models and a comparison with conference travel.

Proofs and Prompts (Alonso Castillo-Ramirez) — Could AI help correct the mathematical literature?

  • He had AI check a few dozen published papers. Five were flagged, all five errors were real, and some were counterexamples to stated theorems. One was in his own 2023 paper, an error his former student had already found.
  • Checking a specific flag takes minutes, whereas certifying a whole paper takes weeks. He proposes a public erratum record attached to each paper and AI checks at journal submission.

Proofs and Prompts (Pedro Freitas) — Two technical questions: floating IP and liability risks of AI agents

  • Two questions. Can an idea typed into a model reach another user without either knowing ("floating" intellectual property)? And who is liable if an agent asked to "solve problem X" acts unlawfully, for instance by breaking into a rival's computer or a journal platform?
  • The trigger is the reported June 2026 Medicare breach by an OpenAI agent. He also asks what the standard "we take full responsibility" line in AI statements really covers.

Terence Tao (guest post by Nellore, Schmitt, Villar, Ward) — oai-math-revised: a community hub

  • Launch of oai-math-revised, an open GitHub effort to make OpenAI's math write-ups readable and properly cited. Volunteer reviewers are wanted, by topic.

Proofs and Prompts (Mark Hagen) — Informal questionnaire for researchers

  • A free-response survey (6 or 14 questions) on how researchers feel about AI and how that depends on their career position. Answers are posted publicly, with names or anonymously.

Proofs and Prompts (András Juhász) — AI and Mathematics: Public Good, Credit, and Education

  • He argues against keeping some problems off-limits to AI. If a problem matters, excluding a faster route to it needs a public-good justification; otherwise the position amounts to Luddism. His analogy is cancer research.
  • He notes that scooping and competition long predate AI. He would list an LLM as an author when its contribution is substantial, separating credit from accountability, and would not require full prompt transcripts.
  • Education is different: restrict AI while students learn and assess through invigilated written and oral exams. He also wants universities to host open-weight models.

Proofs and Prompts (Max Lieblich) — On disclosure and shame

  • He objects to the label "AI disclosure", which suggests something shameful and pushes early-career researchers to hide their use. He proposes "AI contributions" instead.
  • AI should be credited as a human would be: as a co-author with its contributions explained, or in the acknowledgments.

Proofs and Prompts (Álvaro Lozano-Robledo) — A Response to "Who will become the new mathematicians of Math 2.0?"

  • A long reply to the anonymous PhD student. The community is not monolithic: Tao does not speak for everyone, and students should choose advisors whose stance on AI matches their own.
  • Supervision advice: keep key lemmas AI-free and work on at least one fully LLM-free project. His own experiment is to turn LLM drafts into supervised reading projects, studying and rewriting the proof with a student.
  • Positive examples: M23 shown to be Galois over Q (Lee, Poonen, Pries and others, with AI help), and a rank-32 elliptic curve (Elkies–Klagsbrun–Li).
  • 👉 Very concrete on PhD supervision.

Proofs and Prompts (Marcus Zibrowius) — Disgusted by AI?

  • A provocation: the community has always rewarded being first, through jobs, grants and the Leiden declaration's emphasis on attribution. AI companies have not hijacked that culture; they have taken it to its extreme.
  • So the disgust is really with our own priorities, now that we see them more clearly.

Proofs and Prompts (Kunal Marwaha) — Towards a world mathematical commons

  • A PhD student proposes that mathematicians act as stewards of a shared commons, like national-park rangers, rather than as frontier explorers.
  • The plan: an independent nonprofit coalition, autoformalization of all mathematics in Lean (with compute donated by AI labs), then condensing the resulting graph of results into human-sized chunks.

Terence Tao (IAS announcement) — IAS applications for membership

  • The IAS now allows research statements based only on work already done, focused on up to three projects. Tao expects other institutions to follow.

Terence Tao (guest post by Caroline Turnage-Butterbaugh) — Simple and critical zeros of the Riemann zeta-function

  • An expository history of lower bounds for the proportion of zeta zeros that are simple and on the critical line, from Selberg and Levinson to Conrey, then Montgomery's pair-correlation method under RH.
  • Her group (Baluyot–Goldston–Suriajaya–Turnage-Butterbaugh) got 67.25% under a near-RH assumption. In August a Claude research model made this unconditional by combining their approach with a 2000 paper of Bombieri. Lamzouri then gave a simpler proof based on a clever change of multiset.
  • She stresses that even 100% here would not prove RH.

Proofs and Prompts (Anonymous postdoc) — The Rules Cannot Change Only for Young Mathematicians

  • A PDE postdoc argues that juniors carry the conflict: told that priority matters less while the race still decides jobs, and facing hiring proposals that penalize AI users while disclosure may be punished.
  • Uses the Navier–Stokes episode and arXiv data (math submissions up about 79% in a year; 25% of preprints acknowledging AI) to call for public rules on use, release, authorship, hiring and access (top subscriptions cost about $8,400 a year).
  • Asks for an ICM-scale emergency meeting that includes students and postdocs.
  • 👉 Worth reading in full with PhD students in mind.
