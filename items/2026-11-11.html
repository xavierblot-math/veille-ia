Proofs and Prompts (Antonio E. Porreca) — Why we should reject AI in academia

  • A maître de conférences at Aix-Marseille argues that AI-generated papers, prompted by OpenAI's release of 722 such "papers" vetted by an advisory group (AGMAI), should not be treated as legitimate research.
  • His case: these texts ignore academic norms (citing, not scooping, collaborating, writing in good faith), and researchers have a right, even a duty, not to cite or referee them.
  • Main worry is the pipeline: if students and juniors lean on LLMs, no new experts will form, and current experts will lose their skills over time.
  • He also calls on colleagues doing research on generative LLMs to switch fields.
  • 👉 A sharp anti-AI position from inside the French community, with direct points about students and PhD training.

Proofs and Prompts (Jonathan Noel) — I’m giving my theorems away

  • Noel (Victoria) describes a wasteful race: many people get the same AI proof of an open problem, keep it secret, and rush to post it on arXiv first.
  • He traces this to three habits: publishing only polished drafts, treating co-authorship as sacred, and always crediting whoever generated the proof.
  • His experiment, proofsandpeople.org, lists AI-generated proofs not yet understood by humans as open "projects" with a leader and a team who digest them and write them up with shared credit. He seeded it with 10 projects and is giving 8 of them away.
  • He also has a cost reason: he dropped from a $200 to a $20 ChatGPT plan. Links to related essays by Litt, Kra, Trombetti and Morris.

Proofs and Prompts (Carla Cederbaum) — Quo vadis, Mathematics?

  • A Tübingen differential geometer (mathematical relativity) argues that human-to-human mentoring, feedback and peer support are what the AI debate overlooks.
  • Practical ideas: students defend homework orally in front of a TA, "ask a human first" study rooms, more interactive classes, and local panels on when AI use helps learning.
  • She also asks mathematicians to explain publicly why mathematics and trained mathematicians matter, partly to protect teaching and research funding. Also published in the DMV Mitteilungen.
  • 👉 Useful concrete ideas for teaching and PhD supervision.
