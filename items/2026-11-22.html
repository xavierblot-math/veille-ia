Kevin Buzzard (Xena) — Lean for the working mathematician

  • Guest post by Mitchell Taylor (Oxford, PDE / Banach lattices), based on his Imperial PDE seminar talk; Buzzard notes he would not have believed this picture of 2026 two years ago.
  • Story: about 10 hours of a Lean course (taught by Jaume de Dios Pont) were enough for Taylor to check LLM-written formalizations; by March 2026 two joint papers were in Lean, and over a dozen people in his community have since verified their papers. David Muñoz-Lahoz built a Banach lattice library; Lukas Liehr went from zero to millions of lines of Lean in weeks.
  • Uses: Lean "checkpoints" for the decoupled pieces of a 100-page paper, step-by-step generalization of a theorem, letting the LLM glue the pieces and track constants, certifying fast learning of new tools (GPT-5.4 suggested Chern classes to disprove a time-frequency conjecture; the disproof was then autoformalized), and catching a wrong reference that an LLM "solution" relied on.
  • Caveats: you can now verify without understanding; Lean's proof irrelevance ignores what makes different proofs valuable; the reward system favours solving named conjectures over building the theory after. He expects most of mathematics to be formalized within three years and points to a step-by-step tutorial for formalizing papers with a frontier model.
  • 👉 Concrete, practitioner-level workflow from a non-formalist; the tutorial is worth a look if you want to try verifying one of your own papers.

Proofs and Prompts (Florian Lengyel) — On Premature Discovery

  • A short satirical reductio: if AI is blamed for solving a problem before the theory it might have motivated was developed, human solvers deserve the same charge.
  • Examples: Lander–Parkin's 1966 computer refutation of Euler's sum-of-powers conjecture, and OpenAI's reported Lean-formalized results (Sogge's local smoothing conjecture, a refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, 4D Yang–Mills existence).
  • The ironic conclusion: by this logic, the great problem-solvers stand condemned.

Proofs and Prompts (AHM communications group) — AHM Statement on the Announced Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

  • Responding to the 20 November announcement by a mathematician at Anthropic of an AI-generated Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, the AHM argues that no human can currently take responsibility for, or understand, the document.
  • It frames such proofs as a corporate value system that puts formal validity above accountability and understanding, and treats milestones like RH as publicity for superintelligence efforts.
  • It calls for democratic oversight of AI companies and urges mathematicians to keep their research independent of AI models and their owners. Also posted on the AHM website.
  • 👉 A sharp institutional reaction to the RH/GRH news from yesterday's digest; useful context for the debate in the community.
