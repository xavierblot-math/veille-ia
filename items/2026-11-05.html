Terence Tao — More of my interviews and talks

  • A reference list of Tao's recent media pieces on maths and AI. He notes that most posts on his blog are now guest posts, and that his own views are collected on a separate page.
  • Highlights: a Nature World View op-ed calling for a "Maths 2.0" (22 Oct), an Observer interview (23 Oct), Science Friday (28 Oct), a New Scientist piece on the AI race to solve a famous problem (28 Oct), and Rivka Galchen's New Yorker feature "The Year Math Changed Forever" (1 Nov).
  • Also a SAIR Foundation conversation with Yu Deng on open math models. Tao discloses that some of this press is tied to his new book, out on 10 November.
  • 👉 The Nature op-ed and the New Yorker piece are the most substantial if you want his current position in full.

Proofs and Prompts (Anonymous postdoc) — Reject the Neurosis, and Terrible Messaging, of our Loudest Voices — The Case for (Ir)rational Optimism

  • A plea to senior mathematicians to tone down apocalyptic statements to the press. The author argues that this rhetoric is poor leadership and is seriously hurting the morale of PhD students and postdocs.
  • Argues that AI will push the field away from prestige-laden classical conjectures, which the author welcomes. It will also flood the literature with incremental "slop" papers and make papers a weaker signal of ability.
  • Hopes evaluation will shift towards chalk talks, exams and conversation, and that a PhD will again certify the ability to teach core undergraduate and graduate courses.
  • 👉 Relevant to PhD supervision and to how seniors talk to students about AI.

Proofs and Prompts (Anna Maltsev) — In Defence of Incremental Mathematics

  • Part of the series on publishing and credit. A random-matrix probabilist (QMUL) argues that incremental work is the brickwork that breakthroughs need, using the Ricci-flow results that led to Perelman as her example. She adds that it is how early-career people get trained and credited.
  • She reports being two to three times more productive with ChatGPT, and expects the volume of papers to grow by the same factor.
  • Proposal, first raised at the Royal Society AI meeting: split top journals into a flagship plus five online "daughter" journals, on the Nature model, and pay referees £100 per report, with AI checking the routine correctness.
  • An AI-generated cost estimate gives about £2.1M a year (plus £0.5M to launch) for ten journal families, including Annals, Inventiones, Publ. IHÉS and Ann. ENS. Her stance: "Keep calm and carry on", reinforce the system rather than tear it down.

Proofs and Prompts (Joachim von zur Gathen) — Picasso rephrased: AI is useless

  • A very short note built on a (possibly apocryphal) Picasso line: computers only give answers.
  • Its point is that OpenAI's 6 October post attacks the open problems that have guided mathematics. The open question is whether AI will ever pose new problems as fruitful as the old ones.
