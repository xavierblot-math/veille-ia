Terence Tao (guest post by Gireeja Ranade) — AI, Mathematics, Education, and a public goods problem

  • Ranade (EECS professor, Berkeley side of optimization/probability) starts from students demoralised after the Navier–Stokes announcement and asks not whether mathematics survives, but whether ordinary mathematicians and the pipeline that trains them do.
  • Core argument: PhD programmes and entry-level jobs are like "being paid to go to the gym"; if AI removes them, society loses its means of keeping human reasoning in shape — a public goods problem no individual student will solve.
  • Education is the "canary in the coal mine": students handing in proofs they don't understand foreshadow researchers publishing results they don't understand; even with Lean, someone must judge whether formal statements are faithful.
  • Proposals: teach harder rather than shallower, fund 1-1 tutoring, and ask university leaders and funders to publicly commit to hiring and to open-ended theoretical research.
  • 👉 Directly relevant to PhD supervision and to arguing for positions in theoretical fields.

Proofs and Prompts (Luise Ge) — The (human) emotional part of doing maths

  • A CS PhD student (WashU St Louis), replying to Shmuel Weinberger, lists the emotions mathematics gives her, from euphoria and camaraderie to isolation and bewilderment.
  • Her serious point: feelings such as surprise or a sense of "rightness" steer discovery, which she sees as what current AI still lacks; she warns that working with AI may make those feelings shallower.
  • Draws on Turing's "sub-critical vs super-critical minds" passage, raised in a Stephen Muggleton keynote at a reasoning-and-learning conference.

Proofs and Prompts (Jarek Kędra) — Axioms v.1.0.

  • The Aberdeen professor proposes three minimal, Thurston-inspired principles to guide decisions: mathematics exists to deepen human understanding, advances only through it, and independent human thought must be preserved.

Proofs and Prompts (Charles Bordenave) — Mathematics Year Zero

  • The CNRS research director (Aix-Marseille) describes OpenAI's announcement of 372 problems solved across 719 manuscripts as a "bombardment" of theses and careers, and fears falling hiring from 2027 and a collapse of the discipline's appeal to students.
  • Proposals: mandatory transparency on AI use; a boycott of OpenAI tools for research, with an explicit statement in papers; pressure on companies to adopt the Leiden Declaration and the Advisory Group's recommendations; an open-source model controlled by academia.
  • Also: defend positions for young mathematicians regardless of their AI use, invest in AI safety, and recognise a "right to non-use" of AI at work. Written in French, title after Rossellini's Germany Year Zero.
  • 👉 A strong position from the French community, likely to be discussed among colleagues.
