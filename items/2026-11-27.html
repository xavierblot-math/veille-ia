Proofs and Prompts (authors of The Critical 2D KPZ Process) — AI and collaboration for the critical 2D KPZ process

  • The eleven authors of an arXiv paper (22 October 2026) constructing the critical 2D KPZ process, a long-standing problem in their field, explain how generative AI contributed substantially to the result.
  • Timeline: discussions at a May workshop, human insights over the summer, parallel AI experiments that stalled on serious obstacles, then a breakthrough in early September; details are in Appendix C of the paper.
  • Faced with questions of authorship and priority, the people who used AI chose to open the project to the wider community whose decade of prior work made it possible; the group then checked, restructured and often rewrote the proof.
  • They are careful not to generalize, but offer the story as a positive model of collective work rather than a race.
  • 👉 A concrete case study of credit and authorship when AI cracks a problem a community has been working on, relevant to thinking about collaboration norms and supervision.

Proofs and Prompts (Graham Andrew Smith) — Some questions, some answers.

  • Short personal essay by a PUC-Rio associate professor, structured as a series of questions (why write, program, study, teach, do mathematics) with brief answers.
  • Core thesis: writing and programming discipline thought, and delegating our trained "senses" to AI costs us part of ourselves; even if AI could solve everything, scepticism requires having understood how things work.
  • Teaching is framed as the choice between being active participants in the technology or passive consumers.
  • His own reasons for doing mathematics are personal, spiritual (the beauty of truth) and utilitarian, illustrated with historical examples of speculative knowledge later transforming the world. Crossposted from his personal blog.
