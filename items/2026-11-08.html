Proofs and Prompts (Yifu Zhang) — On Expositions, and the Navier–Stokes Generation of PhD Students

  • A first-year numerical analysis PhD student at Imperial describes starting a doctorate right after this year's Navier–Stokes episode, alongside AI results on the Crouzeix conjecture and on matrix-multiplication complexity, and the career anxieties that come with it.
  • A small chart of blog posts by professional mathematicians suggests AI discussion has grown while expository writing has not, despite all the talk about the value of human understanding.
  • Argues for promoting casual, quirky exposition, with Littlewood's A Mathematician's Miscellany as the model: it makes hard mathematics accessible to newcomers, points readers to good sources, supports outreach, and shows junior researchers the field is still alive beyond AI.
  • 👉 A useful view of how incoming PhD students feel this year; relevant to supervision.

Proofs and Prompts (Reuven Peleg) — What Mathematicians Want?

  • A Hebrew University CS master's student notes that the debate so far has been reactive (whether to work with the labs, how journals should treat AI papers) and asks instead what mathematicians actually want from AI.
  • Sketches two camps: those who want AI limited to literature search, spellchecking and coding help, and those who welcome machine-discovered mathematics once reliability, attribution and responsibility are settled.
  • Short post that ends with an open call for mathematicians to describe their ideal outcome, so that AI-for-math researchers build something useful.
