Terence Tao — The mathematical sciences in an AI-enabled world

  • A short pointer to a new report from the UK's Academy for the Mathematical Sciences on how AI is changing mathematical research and its applications.
  • Its central message: as AI takes over more technical work, humans remain essential for choosing questions, supplying key ideas, judging reliability of AI output and building understanding.
  • The report sets near-term and longer-term priorities: education and skills, independent verification, access to AI tools, research assessment, publication and knowledge infrastructure.

Proofs and Prompts (Tim Gehrunger, ETH Zurich) — AI reviews are the new normal

  • Part of the publishing/credit series: argues that an AI error check before posting a preprint is already common practice and should become the norm, including for one's older papers.
  • Evidence from a MathArena study (Dekoninck, Gehrunger, Rögnvaldsson, Sun, Vechev): GPT-6.1 Sol searched arXiv papers for explicit counterexamples to their main theorems, with each hit verified and graded.
  • Major errors turned up in about 6% of 656 papers (Jan 2025–Jul 2026) but only 1.5% of 137 papers from Aug–Sep 2026, when over a quarter of arXiv papers acknowledged AI use — plausibly because authors now pre-check, though AI-written proofs may also be harder for models to fault.
  • Full write-up and data: matharena.ai/literature_mistakes.

Proofs and Prompts (Teo Rema) — Lean me softly: A satire about a dystopian future for mathematics

  • A short story set in 2040: a single company certifies theorems in Lean, prizes reward prompt efficiency, and academia is a caste system of "Prompt Hogs", "Theorem Leeches", "Crumb Pickers" and, lowest of all, "Mathematicians" who still prove things by hand.
  • Satirical threads: an attribution bureau that hands AI firms first authorship over problems their models merely attempted ("buckmastered"), and overproduction so severe that the company funds "Theorem Demand Engineering" to create uses for its theorems.
  • The counterpoint is a topology seminar at a poor university where a proof breaks at the board and the audience leaves happy; the protagonist, a former blogger, ends up sketching on paper again.
  • 👉 A light but pointed read on credit, authorship and the pleasure of doing mathematics; the author notes its debt to Richard Evans Schwartz's story on the Riemann Hypothesis.
