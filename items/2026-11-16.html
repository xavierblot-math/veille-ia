Proofs and Prompts (Orlando Ferrari) — Why will I fight for a PhD in this era? A naive retrospective in the age of AI

  • A master's student (Padova / Paris Dauphine-PSL) explains why he still wants a PhD despite claims that AI has "solved" mathematics.
  • Main argument: the decision should rest on a specific question ("why a PhD on this topic?"), not on AI; the PhD is valued as an apprenticeship, the personal transition from student to independent researcher.
  • On AI: machines still can't reliably verify frontier research and depend on human feedback; he uses AI for notes, tedious inequalities and background reading, but argues a thesis whose ideas are not your own defeats the purpose.
  • Draws heavily on Tao's essays and talks and on 3Blue1Brown's "never a better time to go into math"; the essay was proofread with Gemini.
  • 👉 Useful as a candid student's view for anyone advising PhD candidates right now.

Proofs and Prompts (Aaron Stump) — What do you do with an asshole?

  • A very short parable by a Boston College computer scientist: picture a senior colleague who finds out what young researchers are working on, solves their problems, gives them no credit and just posts the results on a blog.
  • Such a person, he says, would simply be shunned by the community. The implicit target is AI systems or labs taking open problems that early-career researchers are working on.
